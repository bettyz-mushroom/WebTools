Jia's creative process

Interviewed by Betty

  • "When I was studying jewelry design, my creative process usually started with finding a theme that I thought was interesting. The themes were often related to abstract art, and there was usually a question that I wanted to explore or answer through the work I created."

  • "A lot of my inspiration came from visual and cultural references that I found on social media. I also got ideas from objects, artworks, and paintings that I saw in museums. At the beginning of a project, I usually had a vague image or idea in my mind, but it would often change as I continued working."

  • "While creating, I paid a lot of attention to whether the three-dimensional structure and mechanical structure were actually feasible. I needed a working environment where I could both draw by hand and start building with three-dimensional materials. Throughout the process, I made decisions about the proportions and structure of the final piece, as well as the materials I wanted to use."

  • "My direction would often change when I discovered problems while making and testing models. For example, a piece might be uncomfortable to wear, or I might realize that a certain material was not practical or feasible during the making process. These kinds of problems would make me rethink the design and adjust the idea."

  • "I usually considered a piece finished when, most importantly, it looked flawless and when it successfully answered the question that I wanted to explore at the beginning. After finishing the piece, I would photograph it to document what it looked like. I would also keep the sketches and models that I made throughout the process as part of the documentation."
